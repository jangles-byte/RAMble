# Coral

*Design philosophy for the Coral scene — differential growth as the
machine's accretion. Built on the Atelier generative-motion system
reference: a closed chain of nodes that repel locally, stay linked, and
insert new nodes where segments stretch. Brain folds, ruffled reef edges.*

**The job:** make sustained work *accumulate* into form. Where the other
scenes show activity as motion, Coral shows it as growth — the shape on
screen is a fossil record of how hard the machine has been working.

Coral believes computation leaves a residue. A small ring is born in the
dark and grows by insertion: every new node is a moment of work, and the
ruffled, folded perimeter that results is the history of load made visible.
An idle machine's coral creeps; a machine under sustained load blooms folds
on folds. Loading a new model clears the reef and starts a new organism.

Structure is one continuous closed curve, never breaking, folding on itself
like a brain. Memory sets the organism's size budget — RAM fills, the reef
is allowed to grow larger; pressure tightens the folds, as stress shrinks
the personal space between nodes and the curve wrinkles nervously where it
used to billow.

Color is earned by youth. The growing edge — nodes inserted in the last
moments — glows in the theme's palette, and cools to ash-silver as it ages
into the body of the reef. Token streams are growth spurts: while a model
talks, the rim ignites with fresh color faster than it can cool, so
inference reads as a burning coastline. Swap stains the old body red.

**Is not:** a particle effect, symmetric, or ever-shrinking. Kills on
sight: the chain self-intersecting into spaghetti, uniform circular growth
(the dull balloon), hue cycling.
**Signature move:** the shape *is* the history — a viewer who glances twice
an hour watches the machine's day accrete into folds, and a model load
wipes the reef clean like a tide.
