# Fireworks

*Design philosophy for the Fireworks scene — a valley celebration that
scales with the machine's effort. Built on the Atelier generative-motion
craft: envelopes so nothing pops, color earned by event, density with
deliberate negative space.*

**The job:** translate "how hard is the machine working" into the most
universally legible spectacle there is. One glance at the sky says calm
evening or grand finale.

Fireworks believes restraint is what makes spectacle land. The screen is a
night valley: an *invisible* treeline silhouette owns the bottom edge —
never drawn, only implied, because every rocket rises from behind it and
every falling spark dies into it. When a big burst flashes, the sky glows
faintly above the jagged edge and the silhouette appears as negative space,
then vanishes with the light.

Calm is almost still, and that is the point. An idle machine gets one small,
low shell every little while — a lazy summer-night rhythm that makes the
quiet legible. Effort raises everything at once: launch rate, apex height,
burst diameter, variety. Sustained heavy load tips into finale mode —
volleys of three and five, shells stacked high, the sky never fully dark.
The scale from drizzle to finale *is* the gauge.

Color is earned by event, never sprayed: each shell owns one palette color,
sparks cool and dim as they fall, and the crackle flashes are white. Swap
stains shells red as pressure bites. Tokens keep their identity — while a
model streams, extra shells climb in step with generation; a finished
generation earns one enormous golden willow, falling slow.

Variety is a repertoire, not randomness: peonies (the workhorse), rings
(rare, formal), willows (gold, heavy, trailing), and crackles (a beat late,
a scatter of white pops). Weights shift with drive — calm favors small
peonies, the finale unlocks everything.

**Is not:** confetti, a screensaver loop, or busy at idle. Kills on sight:
drawing the treeline as a visible shape, rainbow bursts, constant launches
regardless of load.
**Signature move:** the invisible treeline — fireworks rise from behind a
silhouette you only ever see as negative space during the biggest flashes.
